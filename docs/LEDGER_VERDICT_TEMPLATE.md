# Claim Ledger — Verdict Publication Template (for solicitor review)

Per CLAIM_LEDGER_SPEC.md §Legal guardrails: this template defines the exact
structure of every published verdict. It is submitted for review by a media
solicitor BEFORE first publication of any negative verdict. Nothing outside
this structure is ever published about a claim.

## Structure of a published verdict

Every published verdict consists of, and only of:

1. **Verbatim quote** — an exact substring of the official record
   (Hansard), enforced mechanically at extraction: non-verbatim quotes are
   discarded, never repaired.
2. **Attribution** — speaker name, party, date, and forum ("at Prime
   Minister's Questions, 8 July 2026"), as printed in Hansard.
3. **Source link** — deep link to the contribution on hansard.parliament.uk.
4. **Normalized restatement** — one neutral sentence stating the checkable
   proposition. Screened mechanically against a motive-vocabulary blocklist
   ("lie" and all synonyms of intentional deception); violations are
   discarded before any human sees them.
5. **Verdict** — exactly one of: `Resolved true` / `Resolved false` /
   `Partially true` / `Unresolved`. No other vocabulary exists in the
   system. The words "lie", "liar", "dishonest", "misleading" (as
   characterization) never appear.
6. **Evidence chain** — one or more links to primary public records
   (parliamentary division records at votes.parliament.uk, ONS statistical
   series at ons.gov.uk), each with a factual excerpt stating what the
   record shows. Evidence URLs are constructed by our systems from records
   we fetched — never generated by a language model.

## Editorial controls (defences we build for)

- **Truth (s.2 Defamation Act 2013):** every verdict rests exclusively on
  linked public records; the published excerpt states what the record shows
  in the record's own terms.
- **Honest opinion (s.3):** the verdict label is presented as the operator's
  classification of the claim against the cited records, with the full basis
  displayed for the reader to check.
- **Public interest (s.4):** subject matter is confined to statements made
  by holders of public office in their public capacity, in Parliament or in
  official publications.
- **Human review gate:** no `Resolved false` or `Partially true` verdict can
  be published without named human sign-off. This is enforced by a database
  constraint, not just policy.
- **Right of reply:** any scored figure (verified office) may attach a
  dispute to any claim; disputes display inline with the claim.
- **Corrections:** published, versioned, and fast. Errors acknowledged
  loudly. Any published verdict can be unpublished immediately by an
  operator pending re-review.

## Example (structure only)

> **"We have invested £700 million in probation."**
> — The Deputy Prime Minister, Prime Minister's Questions, 8 July 2026
> [Source: Hansard]
>
> Claim: The Government has invested £700 million in probation.
>
> **Verdict: Resolved true**
>
> Evidence:
> - [official record] — excerpt stating what the record shows.

## Questions for the solicitor

1. Is the fixed verdict vocabulary (`Resolved true/false`, `Partially
   true`, `Unresolved`) defensible as honest opinion / truth, given every
   verdict links its records?
2. Does the "Partially true" label need qualifying copy (e.g. "the records
   support part of this claim") wherever displayed?
3. Is the dispute/right-of-reply mechanism sufficient for the GDPR accuracy
   obligation on per-person scores at the point scores go live (n≥20)?
4. Any required changes to the corrections process before first negative
   verdict publishes?
