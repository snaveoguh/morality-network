# Pilot proposal: two high-street co-ops in Sussex

**From:** Morality Co-operative Ltd
**To:** Sussex & Brighton Strategic Authority (SBSA)
**Status:** Draft for discussion, 20 October 2026. All costs are placeholders.

## Summary

A 12-month pilot supporting one or two community groups in Sussex to take over a high-street shop, pub or service as a co-operative. Co-operatives UK and Plunkett UK provide specialist advice; Morality Co-operative provides the local first contact, the digital and governance tools, and the public dashboard. The pilot tests whether this split works before SBSA commits programme money at scale.

It can start before MHCLG publishes programme rules, using a small amount of SBSA or council money, and be folded into the programme once allocations are known.

## Why a pilot

- SBSA has no co-op team and will not have a mayor until May 2028.
- Programme allocations, rules and timescales are not yet published.
- A small pilot gives SBSA evidence, a working dashboard and two real case studies by the time money arrives.

## Who

### Co-ops (to be selected)

One or two groups, chosen with the constituent councils, from:

- **A pub at risk of closure**, ideally already listed as an Asset of Community Value, in a market town or large village.
- **A vacant high-street unit** turned into a community shop, café or shared workspace, linked to the Derelict Buildings Fund or High Street Rental Auctions where possible.
- **An existing small business whose owner is retiring**, converting to employee or customer ownership.

Selection criteria: a steering group already formed; a realistic site; local support shown by a public meeting or pledges; at least one site in East or West Sussex outside Brighton & Hove, so the pilot is not city-only.

We will not name candidate groups publicly until they agree.

### Partners

| Partner | Role |
|---|---|
| SBSA | Commissioner; sets priorities; owns grant decisions and data |
| Constituent councils (Brighton & Hove, East Sussex, West Sussex) and district councils | Local knowledge, Asset of Community Value listings, property and licensing contacts |
| Co-operatives UK | Model rules, registration route, sector advice, national programme link |
| Plunkett UK | Specialist pub and shop advice (if a pub or shop is selected) |

| Morality Co-operative | Local coordination, digital and governance layer, dashboard, reporting |

## What Morality supplies

### Digital layer
- Member register and share register (secure, exportable, owned by the co-op)
- Online share-offer page with application and payment handover to the co-op's bank or a regulated payment provider (we do not hold funds)
- Online voting for AGMs and special resolutions, with paper and in-person options kept
- A simple public page for each co-op
- Training for two people per co-op to run all of the above without us

### Governance layer
- Plain-English templates: board terms of reference, conflicts register, minutes, member update, annual report
- Facilitation of the first two board meetings and the first AGM
- A first-year governance checklist matched to the FCA's annual return

### Public accountability
- The public dashboard ([dashboard-spec.md](dashboard-spec.md)) live for the pilot co-ops
- Records published through the pooter.world Pledge Ledger
- Quarterly written report to SBSA, published

All software is open source. If the pilot ends, each co-op keeps its data and tools, and SBSA can take the dashboard to another supplier.

## Success measures

| Measure | Target at 12 months |
|---|---|
| Co-ops registered with the FCA | 2 of 2 (or 1 of 1) |
| Community share offers launched | At least 1 |
| Members recruited | [N] per co-op (set with each group) |
| Money raised from members | £[X] per co-op |
| Trading | At least 1 co-op open and trading |
| Paid jobs created or kept | [N] FTE |
| Governance | First AGM held; annual return filed on time; board of at least 5 |
| Self-sufficiency | Each co-op runs its own member register and votes without Morality's help |
| Transparency | Dashboard live, every payment shown within 10 working days |
| Partner satisfaction | SBSA, Co-operatives UK and both groups would recommend continuing (short survey) |

Longer-term: still trading at 1, 3 and 5 years, tracked on the dashboard after the pilot ends.

We will also report what went wrong. A pilot that finds the model does not work is still useful to SBSA.

## Timeline (12 months)

| Months | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0 | Agreement with SBSA; data-sharing agreement and DPIA; introductions to Co-operatives UK |
| 1–2 | Call for groups via councils; select one or two; steering-group support; dashboard skeleton live |
| 2–4 | Legal form and model rules chosen; business plans drafted; specialist advice begins |
| 3–5 | FCA registration; digital tools set up; first board meetings |
| 5–8 | Share offers prepared, reviewed and launched; grant applications |
| 8–10 | Share offers close; purchase or lease; fit-out |
| 10–12 | Opening (target for at least one); first AGM; final report and recommendations to SBSA |

Purchases and openings depend on vendors, licensing and planning, so dates after month 8 are the least certain.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Programme rules, when published, do not fit the pilot | Keep the pilot small and cheap; design reporting to adapt to MHCLG fields |
| Site falls through | Select groups with more than one option, or a second group on standby |
| Share offer under-raises | Realistic targets; practitioner review; staged plan with a lower-cost fallback |
| Volunteer burnout | Board succession planning from month 3; paid staff in the business plan |
| Conflict of interest (pooter.world coverage) | Declared interest statement; no editorial input from SBSA; corrections register |
| Dependence on Morality | Open-source tools; data owned by co-ops; training so groups run tools themselves |
