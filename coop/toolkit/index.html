The positionMoney announced, rules not yet

On 25 September 2026 MHCLG announced a £210m high-streets package. £10m of it is a regional Co-operative Development Programme to help mayors and strategic authorities set up co-operatives owned and run by the staff, customers and communities who use them.

No rules, allocations or timetable have been published. The money comes from existing MHCLG high-streets budgets.

What you get

A plain guide for community groups, a public dashboard that tracks every award and whether each co-op is still trading at one, three and five years, a governance kit for new boards, and a 12-month pilot with one or two Sussex co-ops.

What we don't do

  • Replace Co-operatives UK, Plunkett UK or practitioners
  • Give regulated legal or financial advice
  • Hold or manage grant money for co-ops
  • Take shares, board seats or fees from the co-ops we help

For community groupsFrom shop, pub or service to co-op

  1. Steering group. Public meeting, 5–9 people, written purpose. List the building as an Asset of Community Value if it's at risk.
  2. Legal form. Usually a community benefit society with an asset lock, or a co-operative society. Minimum three members.
  3. Model rules. Use Co-operatives UK or Plunkett UK model rules. Quicker and cheaper than bespoke.
  4. FCA registration. Via the sponsoring body. Appears on the Mutuals Public Register.
  5. Business plan. One a sceptic would believe, including the case where trade is 20% down.
  6. Community shares. Follow the Community Shares Handbook. Consider the Standard Mark. No FSCS or Ombudsman cover; say so.
  7. Open and trade. Licences, insurance, staffing model decided early.
  8. First-year governance. Registers, AGM, FCA annual return within seven months of year end, conflicts register.

Privacy

No personal data on members. Counts under five suppressed. Nothing below ward level except a public premises. No analytics or advertising scripts. Each co-op sees changes to its row before they go live.

Tamper-evident

Each award is recorded in the pooter.world Pledge Ledger. Records are append-only; corrections are added, never overwritten. A daily fingerprint of the records is published so anyone can check nothing was changed after the fact. Nobody needs a crypto wallet to read it.

PilotOne or two Sussex co-ops, 12 months

Candidates

A pub at risk of closure; a vacant high-street unit turned into a shop, café or workspace; or a retiring owner's business moving to employee or customer ownership. At least one outside Brighton & Hove.

Measures

Registered with the FCA; share offer launched; at least one trading; first AGM held and annual return filed on time; dashboard live with every payment shown within 10 working days; co-ops running their own tools without us.

Read thisDeclared interest

Morality Co-operative runs pooter.world, which includes a Pledge Ledger that records and tracks the public pledges of strategic authorities, including SBSA. pooter.world may also report on SBSA, its councils and its spending. If we become a delivery partner we will have a financial relationship with SBSA.

Our commitments: the interest is declared on every relevant page, so every pooter.world item and Pledge Ledger record on SBSA or the programme carries a note of it; people who work on SBSA partnership work do not decide verdicts about SBSA; an outside reviewer checks any verdict about SBSA before publication; SBSA does not see coverage before publication and has no editorial say; corrections go in the public corrections register; our own payments appear on the same dashboard as everyone else's.
