## What we want

Stay the same society. Change the rules so that:

1. Membership opens to three classes: **workers**, **reader-citizens** and **local partners**.
2. Workers keep operational control; readers and partners get a guaranteed voice on the board.
3. An **asset lock** in the rules stops the society's assets being handed to members or sold off.
4. The society can run a **community share offer** of £100k–£250k, with Standard Mark approval and match funding.

Fixed limits we have set ourselves, and which the rules should reflect:

- Shares are **withdrawable and non-transferable**. This keeps the offer within the exemption for society shares described in the [Community Shares Handbook, s.1.2](https://www.uk.coop/resources/community-shares-handbook/1-share-capital/12-share-offers-and-regulation-cs) and keeps any secondary market out of the picture. (The FCA treats a market in shares that allows capital gains as "normally inconsistent with registration": [RFCCBS 6.1.13](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/RFCCBS/6/1.html).)
- **No profit-share tokens**, and **no tradeable token is ever equity** in the society or a claim on its surplus.
- **No acquisition or exit.** The asset lock and its entrenchment clause exist to make that very hard.
- The old **MO token is not part of this** in any form: not a share, not a membership credential, not a reward.

## What changes by rule amendment

All of this can be done by amending the rules of the existing society. No new legal entity is needed **as long as we stay a co-operative society** (see "What's uncertain" below).

### Member classes

| Class | Who | How they join (outline) |
|---|---|---|
| Worker members | People employed by, or regularly contracted to work for, the society | Board admits after a qualifying period; one worker share |
| Reader members | Individuals who use pooter.world and support its objects | Apply and buy at least the minimum shareholding (e.g. £[25]) |
| Partner members | Local organisations: community groups, civic bodies, other co-ops, local media, universities | Apply; board admits; minimum shareholding (e.g. £[250]) |

Detail is in [02-draft-rules-outline.md](02-draft-rules-outline.md).

A multi-stakeholder structure is consistent with the co-operative condition so long as each class has a genuine mutual relationship with the society. The FCA's guidance on what counts as a bona fide co-operative is in [FG15/12](https://www.fca.org.uk/static/documents/fg15-12.pdf) (now carried in the FCA Handbook as [RFCCBS](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/RFCCBS.pdf)). **Check with Co-operatives UK** that reader members, whose relationship is as users of a free public service, satisfy that test.

### Asset lock

As a co-operative society we can write a **non-statutory** asset lock into our rules. It is not as strong as the statutory lock available to community benefit societies: the [Community Shares Handbook](https://www.uk.coop/resources/community-shares-handbook/2-society-legislation-cs) and the FCA both note that a co-operative's rule-based lock "does not have the same effect" as a statutory one. A rule-based lock can in principle be removed by a later rule amendment, so the draft rules add an entrenchment clause (rule 9.4 in [02](02-draft-rules-outline.md): a special resolution at a general meeting, the consent of a majority of each member class voting separately, and the prior written consent of a named external Asset Guardian) to make it hard to undo. **Solicitor check:** how far a co-operative's rules can entrench a clause against future amendment.

The lock covers: no distribution of reserves to members; on dissolution, residual assets go to another asset-locked body with similar objects; no transfer of engagements or conversion to a company unless the receiving body has an equivalent or stronger lock.

### Board make-up

Proposed board of 5–9:

- Worker directors: a majority (e.g. 3 of 5, or 5 of 9), elected by worker members.
- Reader directors: at least 1 (2 on a board of 9), elected by reader members.
- Partner directors: at least 1, elected by partner members.
- Up to 2 co-opted independent directors for skills (finance, media law), non-voting on matters reserved to workers.

Operational matters (hiring, editorial line, product decisions) sit with the worker members and the worker directors. Readers and partners have a guaranteed seat and vote on the board, and must consent to certain reserved matters (asset lock, objects, editorial independence clause, dissolution, the share interest rate).

## The process

1. **Get our current registered rules** from the FCA register or our own records and confirm: the amendment procedure, notice period for general meetings, quorum, and the majority needed for a rule change. In most Co-operatives UK-derived rules this is a special resolution (often two-thirds or three-quarters of votes cast at a general meeting). Confirm the exact threshold in *our* rules before calling the meeting.
2. **Ask Co-operatives UK to act as sponsoring body.** Societies that adopt a sponsor's model rules pay a much reduced FCA fee and get a faster review ([RFCCBS 3.4](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/RFCCBS/3/4.pdf)). The base should be the Co-operatives UK multi-stakeholder co-operative model rules, with as few departures as possible: each departure raises the fee and review time.
3. **Draft the new rules** with Co-operatives UK, using [02-draft-rules-outline.md](02-draft-rules-outline.md) as the brief.
4. **Call a general meeting** under the current rules and pass the rule change as a **special resolution** (or whatever our rules require). Minute the vote.
5. **Submit a complete amendment** (a whole new set of rules replacing the old) through the FCA **[Mutuals Society Portal](https://fca.org.uk/firms/mutual-societies-forms)**. The [complete amendment form](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/forms/complete-amendment-to-rules-1969-and-2014-act.docx) asks for:
   - the completed form;
   - one signed copy of the new rules;
   - a marked-up copy showing changes against the sponsor's model rules;
   - the declarations and signatures the FCA's complete amendment form asks for.
   A [partial amendment](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/forms/partial-amendment-to-rules-1969-and-2014-act.docx) (signed by three members and the secretary) is the alternative for small changes, but this change is large enough that a complete amendment is cleaner.
6. **FCA registers the amendment** if it is not contrary to the Act, and issues a sealed acknowledgement. The rule change has no effect until registered.
7. **Update our public materials** (`/coop` page on pooter.world, membership forms, privacy notice) and open membership.

## What's uncertain

### Switching to a community benefit society: probably not by rule amendment

A community benefit society would give us the **statutory** asset lock under the [Community Benefit Societies (Restriction on Use of Assets) Regulations 2006](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2006/264/contents), which is stronger and is what some funders and the Standard Mark assessor may prefer.

But the [Community Shares Handbook, s.2.6.6](https://www.uk.coop/resources/community-shares-handbook/2-society-legislation/26-converting-legal-forms/266-converting-cs) says that societies registered from 1 August 2014 onwards "cannot convert from one form to another". Only co-operatives registered *before* that date can convert to a community benefit society by rule change. We registered in September 2019. On that reading, becoming a CBS would mean:

- registering a **new** community benefit society, and
- passing a special resolution to **transfer our engagements** to it under [s.110 of the 2014 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/14/part/9/crossheading/amalgamations-and-transfers-of-engagements-between-societies) ([RFCCBS 8.8](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/RFCCBS/8/8.pdf)).

That is more work, and it ends the "same society" continuity we want (new register number, bank account, contracts, grant agreements). It would also change what we are: a CBS must be run for the benefit of the community, not its members, which sits awkwardly with workers keeping operational control.

**Action: write to the FCA Mutual Societies team** to confirm whether any route exists for a post-2014 co-operative to become a CBS without a transfer of engagements, and ask Co-operatives UK what they recommend. Until we hear back, the plan is to **stay a co-operative society with a rule-based asset lock**.

### Other open points

- Whether reader members (users of a free service) satisfy the co-operative condition. See above.
- Whether a rule-based asset lock and entrenchment clause will satisfy Booster / Community Shares ICOF and the Standard Mark assessor.
- Current FCA fee for a complete amendment with and without sponsor model rules (check the [FCA forms page](https://fca.org.uk/firms/mutual-societies-forms) and Co-operatives UK).
- Current FCA turnaround times for complete amendments.
