# 05 — Becoming a reader member: questions and answers

**Status:** draft. Answers depend on the new rules being registered by the FCA ([01](01-conversion-options.md), [02](02-draft-rules-outline.md)). Figures in `[brackets]` are not yet fixed.

---

### What is Morality Co-operative?

A small co-operative that builds **pooter.world**, a free tool for checking what politicians and public bodies say and promise. It is registered with the Financial Conduct Authority as a co-operative society (no. 4621). Until now it has been owned only by the three people who work in it. We are changing our rules so that readers and local organisations can become members too.

### What is a reader member?

A person who uses pooter.world, supports what it does, and owns at least one small share in the co-operative. Reader members get a vote, elect directors to the board, and get regular reports on how the money is spent.

### Who else can be a member?

There are three kinds of member:

- **Worker members:** the people who work for the co-operative.
- **Reader members:** individuals, like you.
- **Partner members:** local organisations, such as community groups, other co-ops, local news outlets and universities.

Political parties and campaigns can't be partner members.

### How do I join?

Apply online at `[pooter.world/coop/join]` and buy the minimum shareholding of £`[25]`. You need to be 16 or over. Once the board accepts your application, you are a member.

### How much does it cost?

The minimum is £`[25]`. There is no yearly fee. During a share offer you can put in more, up to £`[25,000]`, but you get **one vote however much you put in**.

### What do I get?

- One vote at general meetings, including on the annual accounts and big decisions.
- A vote for the reader directors on the board.
- Annual accounts, a yearly report and quarterly updates.
- An invitation to member meetings, online and in person.
- `[Planned: the chance to suggest claims and pledges to check, and to join reader review panels.]`

### Do I get a say in what pooter.world publishes?

No, and that's deliberate. Our rules say no member, funder, partner or investor can tell the editorial team what to publish or block anything. That includes reader members and it includes the people who buy the most shares. You get a say in **how the co-operative is run**, not in what it says about anyone. If you think we got something wrong, use the corrections process, which is open to everyone.

### Who actually runs it day to day?

The workers. Worker members keep control of daily work: hiring, the product, and editorial decisions within the published editorial policy. Readers and partners have guaranteed seats on the board, and certain decisions need their agreement: changing what the co-operative is for, changing the asset lock, changing the editorial independence rule, setting the interest rate cap, or closing the co-operative.

### How are votes counted?

Each member has one vote. At general meetings the votes of each class are scaled so that workers count for `[50]`%, readers `[30]`% and partners `[20]`% of the total, however many members there are in each. This stops any one group swamping the others as membership grows.

### Can I lose my money?

**Yes.** If the co-operative fails you could lose some or all of what you paid. Your shares are **not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme**, and you **can't take a complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service**. Only put in what you can afford to be without. ([Community Shares Handbook on consumer protection](https://www.uk.coop/resources/community-shares-handbook/7-regulation-and-guidance/726-consumer-protection-cs))

### Can I get my money back?

Usually yes, but not straight away and not guaranteed. Shares bought in a share offer can't be withdrawn for the first `[3]` years, except in hardship or on death. After that you give `[3]` months' notice. You get back what you paid, or less if the co-operative has made losses. The board can delay or suspend withdrawals if paying them would put the co-operative at risk. This is normal for community shares ([RFCCBS 6.1](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/RFCCBS/6/1.html)).

### Can my shares go up in value?

No. They can never be worth more than you paid. That's part of what makes this a co-operative rather than an investment.

### Do I get interest?

Maybe, from year `[3]`, if the co-operative can afford it, at up to `[2]`% a year. The cap in our rules is `[3]`%. Interest isn't a share of profits and missed years aren't made up later.

### Can I sell or give away my shares?

No. Shares can't be sold or transferred, except to your estate when you die. If you want out, you withdraw them on the terms above.

### Is there a token, NFT or crypto version of membership?

No. Your membership and shares are recorded in our register of members, like any co-operative. **No token or digital asset represents shares, votes or any right to the co-operative's money, and none ever will.** We publish some records on a public blockchain so anyone can check they haven't been altered, but those records give nobody any rights in the co-operative. The old MO token has nothing to do with membership.

### Could the co-operative be sold, or could the founders cash out?

No. Our new rules include an **asset lock**: the co-operative's assets can't be shared out among members or sold off. If it ever closes, anything left after paying its debts and repaying members' shares goes to another organisation with similar aims and its own asset lock. Changing the asset lock would need every class of member to agree and an outside guardian's consent.

### What happens to my personal details?

We keep your name, address and shareholding in our register of members, as the law requires. We use your email to send member notices and reports. We don't sell or share your details. `[Link to privacy notice.]`

### Do I have to declare anything?

If you stand for the board, yes: party membership, political work and any links to bodies we cover go on a public register. Ordinary reader members don't need to declare anything, but please tell us if you are applying on behalf of a political party or campaign: they can't be members.

### Can I stop being a member?

Yes. Withdraw your shares (on the terms above) and your membership ends.

### Who regulates the co-operative?

The FCA registers co-operative societies and keeps our rules and annual returns on the [Mutuals Public Register](https://mutuals.fca.org.uk/Search/Society/30459). It does not regulate share offers like ours. If we run a share offer, we will apply for the [Community Shares Standard Mark](https://communityshares.org.uk/community-shares-standard-mark), which shows the offer meets national good-practice standards. It is not a guarantee.
